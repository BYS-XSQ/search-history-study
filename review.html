Search history on Google

A desktop design concept for quickly revisiting and managing searches without leaving the homepage.

This prototype uses fictional searches and local browser storage. It does not access or change your Google account. Voice search is represented visually.

Design rationale

Search history opens directly on the Google homepage, keeping the user in the context of search. A secondary entry beside the existing search buttons makes the feature discoverable without competing with the primary search action. Existing recent-search suggestions also lead to the full view. The non-modal popover leaves the homepage layout unchanged and closes on an outside click, its close button, or Escape; a link to My Activity preserves access to advanced account controls.

The expanded panel uses compact, three-column cards grouped by date, ordered from newest to oldest across each row. Search terms carry the strongest typographic emphasis; service labels and times sit on a quieter second line. Restrained borders, small service icons and consistent alignment separate items without the weight of a dashboard. Query, date and Marked filters support retrieval as history grows. A resistant leftward drag reveals Mark and Delete; a three-dot button and keyboard provide equivalent access. Dragging never deletes a search directly. Opening a past query repeats that search in a new tab, preserving the user's position in history.

Saving and deleting are separate actions. Turning off history stops future saving while keeping existing records available for review and deletion. The off state explains this behavior and provides a direct way to turn saving back on. Deleting either one search or all searches requires confirmation because Google documents deletion as irreversible. The delete-all confirmation explicitly includes records outside the current filter and explains whether future searches will still be saved. Empty and no-match states provide distinct explanations. Advanced privacy and other service settings remain in My Activity.
